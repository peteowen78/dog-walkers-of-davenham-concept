Can people find Dog Walkers of Davenham online?

We looked at your website the way Google and a first-time visitor do. Here is what we found, in plain English, with no jargon.

4 of 10

Your site passes 4 of the 10 basic checks. Google can find you, and the site fits a phone. Six gaps are holding it back, and all six are fixable.

The one that matters most

Browsers tell visitors your site is "Not secure".

Modern websites use a secure connection, shown by a padlock and an address starting "https". Yours does not have one. When we asked for the secure version, the connection was refused.

Browsers such as Chrome label sites like this "Not secure" in the address bar. Nothing dangerous is happening, but for a business that asks people to hand over their dog and their house keys, it is the wrong first impression.

Search engines also prefer secure sites, and a secure connection is normally free to set up.

Five smaller things to fix

None of these is hard. Together they decide how you look in search results and when someone shares your link.

To fix

There is no description for Google to show

Under the blue link in a search result there are two lines of text. Your site does not supply any, so Google has to pick some words from the page itself.

A new site would say it for you: small group dog walks around Davenham with Ann and Andy, never more than four dogs.

To fix

Shared links are titled "Home"

When a customer recommends you on Facebook or WhatsApp, a picture does appear, but the title beside it reads "Home" and there is no description. Recommendations are how dog walkers find new customers.

A new site would put your name and one clear line on every share.

To fix

The main heading is blank

Every page has one main heading, and search engines read it first. On your home page it exists but is empty.

A new site would open with a heading that says who you are and where you walk.

To fix

Four of your ten pictures have no description

Each picture can carry a short written description. One of yours reads "No photo description available".

A new site would describe every picture in plain words.

To fix

Google is not handed your details

Websites can give Google a small fact sheet: what you do, the area you cover and your phone number. Yours has none.

A new site would include that fact sheet, marked up as a local dog walking service.

Can AI assistants find you?

More people now ask ChatGPT, Claude or Google's AI answers instead of searching. These tools read your website too. We checked four things they rely on. Your site passes 3 of 4.

Working

AI assistants are allowed in

Nothing on your site turns them away.

Working

The words can be read without running the page

Your words are there in the page itself, so AI tools can read them.

Working

Your phone number and address are written out

Both are written on your home page.

To fix

A summary file for AI tools

There is none. It is a newer idea, a short plain-text summary of who you are and the area you cover, and it takes about twenty minutes to write.

Some AI tools are also less keen on sites without a secure connection, which is one more reason to fix that first.

Nobody can make an AI recommend a business, and anyone who offers to is selling nothing. What a website can do is let these tools reach it, read it and get your facts right.

What we would do

People choosing a dog walker are deciding who to trust. A site that browsers mark "Not secure" works against the good reviews you already have. A new site would fix all six points above.
